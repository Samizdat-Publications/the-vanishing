## Executive Summary

This is the most consequential sweep the project has run, and most of what it found cuts against the pattern hypothesis.

**Three headline findings:**

1. **No new cases originated in the window.** Zero deaths or disappearances between June 20 and August 7, 2026 meet the inclusion criteria. Four independent trackers with July update stamps agree. One pre-window name (James "Tony" Moffatt, April 17, 2026) surfaced that prior sweeps missed and requires an inclusion decision.

2. **The tracked data contains material errors.** At least eight of the fourteen case records have incorrect dates, locations, occupations, or circumstance details when checked against primary sources. Several of these errors make cases look more anomalous than the record supports. These must be corrected before v2 is built.

3. **The skeptical case has consolidated and is stronger than the tracker currently reflects.** Between April 20 and May 7, 2026, roughly twenty mainstream outlets and four independent statistical analyses converged on "unconnected." President Trump publicly reversed on May 1. No outlet has retracted, because none had made affirmative factual claims. The counterarguments section of the tracker currently understates this substantially.

**What this does not mean.** Four missing-person files remain genuinely open (Reza, Chavez, Garcia, McCasland), one death remains without a public cause (Maiwald), and one has no cause of death determination more than a year on (Casias). The project retains value as a rigorous, well-sourced case tracker. What the evidence does not support is the connective thesis.

## Section 4: The Skeptical and Statistical Case

The tracker's counterarguments section currently lists five sources. The actual body of skeptical work is larger, more quantitative, and stronger than the tracker reflects. Presented here in its strongest form, per RESEARCH-PROCESS.md quality standards.

### 4.1 Base-rate analyses

Four independent quantitative attacks exist. They use different denominators and do not agree with each other numerically, which is itself worth recording.

**Steven Novella, NeuroLogica Blog, April 21, 2026 -- the strongest of the four:**

| Measure | Figure |
|---|---|
| US researchers | ~2 million |
| Baseline mortality | ~25 deaths per million per day |
| Expected scientist deaths over 4 years | **~73,000** |
| Cases on the alleged list | 11 |
| Gap | "far less than random noise, by at least two orders of magnitude" |
| JPL (~4,500 employees), expected deaths over 4 years | **~164**. List contains 5. |
| Los Alamos (~18,000 employees), expected deaths over 4 years | **~657**. List contains 2-3. |

**The JPL and LANL sub-analyses are the key move and the tracker must engage with them.** The clusters the pattern narrative treats as most suspicious are the exact places where the observed count is most dramatically **below** the expected baseline. If a foreign service were killing JPL staff, JPL's count should exceed baseline, not sit at roughly 3% of it.

**David Hand (statistician, Imperial College), via Faye Flam, Scientific American, May 7, 2026:**
- ~70,000 people work in nuclear-energy-related fields
- ~200,000 adults go missing annually in the US
- Derived: roughly **50 people per year** involved in nuclear work would be expected to go missing on general-population rates
- Hand: "Random numbers, words or distributions of events can appear to clump and cluster in patterns if you make the numbers big enough."
- Hand's verdict: "I don't really think there's a mystery or, indeed, anything suspicious that needs to be explained."
- Named fallacies: the **near-enough effect** (expanding the definition until the pattern fits) and the **look-elsewhere effect** (finding clusters post hoc rather than by pre-specified criteria)

**Mick West, via Vanity Fair, April 22, 2026:** >700,000 people hold top-secret-cleared positions in US aerospace and nuclear sectors. Against normal mortality, ~**250** would be expected to die by homicide or suicide over the relevant period, with thousands more of natural causes.

**Michael Shermer, Skeptic, April 25, 2026:** 27,300 US gun-suicide deaths/year; 1,200-1,600 disappearances/year in US National Parks; >500,000 missing-person reports/year (FBI); ~50,000 suicides/year; ~7,500 pedestrian traffic deaths/year.

### 4.2 Structural arguments

**Shermer's "fallacy of excluded exceptions"** -- a 2x2 matrix: (1) UFO/defense-linked scientists who died or vanished; (2) UFO/defense-linked scientists who did not; (3) non-defense scientists who died or vanished; (4) non-defense scientists who did not. The narrative examines only Cell 1. This is a clean and correct statement of the problem and the tracker should address it directly.

**Daniel Engber, The Atlantic, April 22, 2026:** "To call it a conspiracy theory would be far too kind, because no comprehensive theory has been floated to explain the pattern of events." Engber's specific point -- that the **absence of a proposed mechanism, actor, or motive** is disqualifying -- has never been answered by pattern-side coverage.

**Rep. James Walkinshaw (D-VA), the motive argument:** "The United States has thousands of nuclear scientists and nuclear experts. It's not the kind of nuclear program that potentially a foreign adversary could significantly impact by targeting 10 individuals."

**Jennifer Coffindaffer, retired FBI Special Agent, Newsweek, April 27, 2026 -- the direct rebuttal to the "left everything behind" thread:**
- "It is easy to construct conspiracies out of raw data when individual cases are stripped of personal context."
- "It's not just what they leave behind that's important. Even more important is what they choose to take."
- "Essentially, they are saying goodbye to their life and they don't need any of those things."

Her argument: leaving wallet, phone and keys behind is a **known signature of suicidal intent**, not of abduction. Investigators read the evidence the opposite way from how the pattern narrative reads it. She also notes the cases lack the operational consistency of a real coordinated program: different disciplines, institutions, and clearance levels.

**Robert Bartholomew, Psychology Today, April 22, 2026 -- apophenia:** "the tendency to see meaningful links in unrelated events," compounded by confirmation bias. "Deaths in large populations are inevitable; out of such a large pool, some people will die unexpectedly, have accidents, or go missing for reasons unrelated to their work." On social media: postings "plant the idea that there has been a coordinated attack on American scientists," priming people "to reinterpret random deaths and disappearances as suspicious and sinister." **No newer Bartholomew writing found after April 22, 2026.**

### 4.3 Fact-checks

- **Snopes**, April 28, 2026: the idea of a conspiracy is "purely conjecture."
- **PolitiFact**, April 28, 2026: tracked **14 people**; found **not all were scientists**; the claim that "nearly all worked together" is unsupported (employers spanned NASA, LANL, USAF, MIT, Caltech and private firms); no public confirmation any accessed classified UFO or nuclear-weapons programs. Specific corrections: Casias was an administrative assistant, Chavez a retired construction foreman, Hicks studied comets and asteroids, Grillmair studied dark matter and exoplanets. Sullivan: accidental drug overdose.
- **FBI / USAO-Massachusetts, April 29, 2026:** Loureiro determination (see 2.7).

### 4.4 Where the skeptics overreach -- log for balance

1. **Scientific American concedes its own gap.** The piece explicitly states it is "impossible to find statistics on the expected number of scientists and science-related people who were shot or disappeared within a given time period in the U.S." Its argument is heuristic, not a calculation. Flam's framing is more confident than the underlying math supports. Novella's institution-level analysis is stronger precisely because it supplies a matched denominator.
2. **Shermer compresses the Ning Li facts** (see 2.11).
3. **Shermer's ">500,000 missing yearly"** is the all-ages NCIC figure, heavily juvenile and mostly resolved within days, deployed in an argument about adult professionals. Hand's 200,000-adults figure is the correct one. A reader comparing the two would think the skeptics contradict each other.
4. **The Pentagon's early response was thin.** Its statement that there are "no active national security investigations" of missing persons with clearances was weak enough that Comer and Burlison said it left "many unanswered questions." Reflexive agency dismissal is not a finding.
