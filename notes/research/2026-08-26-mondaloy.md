# Mondaloy, Monica Jacinto Reza, and the alleged McCasland link

**Run 26 August 2026.** Commissioned after Stewart flagged the alloy's history as worth a
deeper dive. The headline finding cuts against the tracker's framing and is recorded as a
finding, per the project's standard.

## Verdict

**The alleged Reza-McCasland connection does not survive his own service biography.** The
patent record is real, small, entirely public, and contains **no government-interest
clause**. No US patent was ever granted. Reza is correctly described as co-inventor and
first-named of two, but "lead inventor" is not a category the patent record recognises, and
the two most-repeated biographical claims about her are unsupported at source.

## The patent record

Google Patents full-text search for `"mondaloy"` returns exactly two results, both Florida
Turbine Technologies, neither naming the inventors. **The trade name appears in no patent by
the inventors.** The alloy is patented under a generic title.

| Field | US20030053926A1 | US20040208777A1 | US20100266442A1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title | Burn-resistant and high tensile strength metal alloys | same | same |
| Application | 09/954,835 | 10/769,195 | 12/626,205 |
| Filed | 18 Sep 2001 | 30 Jan 2004 | 25 Nov 2009 |
| Priority | 18 Sep 2001 | 18 Sep 2001 | 18 Sep 2001 |
| Inventors | Monica A. Jacinto; Dallis Ann Hardwick | same | same |
| Granted | **No** | **No** | **No** |
| Status | Abandoned, failure to respond | Abandoned | Abandoned |
| Government-interest clause | **None** | **None** | **None** |

**The government-interest clause is established as ABSENT, not merely unfound.** Raw HTML for
all three publications was retrieved and the complete document text searched for
"government", "federally sponsor", "certain rights", "under contract" and "Air Force". Zero
matches in all three, on all five terms. Confirmed a second way by heading order: a
`STATEMENT REGARDING FEDERALLY SPONSORED RESEARCH` section conventionally sits between the
cross-reference and field-of-invention headings, and in all three it is simply not there.

**Consequence: the patent record names no funding agency, no contract number and no
government rights. Anyone citing a Mondaloy government-interest clause is citing something
that does not exist.**

**The innocent explanation must be carried alongside.** Priority date is September 2001;
Reza has said the AFRL cost-share began in 1999. If the invention as filed was conceived on
company funds, the absence is unremarkable rather than anomalous. That reading is not
confirmed here.

**A granted patent exists in Russia and never existed in the United States.**
`RU2301276C2`, filed 17 Sep 2002, **granted 20 June 2007**, assignee United Technologies
Corporation, now lapsed. Recorded as a bare record fact. The ordinary explanation is
prosecution and assignment mechanics at a company operating internationally, and nothing
here establishes more than that.

Assignment chain: Jacinto and Hardwick to **The Boeing Company** (2001), to **Ruby
Acquisition Enterprises** by correction, to **Pratt and Whitney Rocketdyne** by name change,
to **Aerojet Rocketdyne of DE**. That is the ordinary corporate history of the Rocketdyne
business unit, not evidence of anything unusual.

## The programme

From the patent face: nickel-based, roughly 55 to 75 percent nickel, tensile strength at
least 145,000 psi while self-extinguishing at oxygen partial pressures at or above 4,000
psi. The "strong and burn-resistant in oxygen-rich environments" characterisation is
**confirmed from the patent itself**, not merely from press coverage.

Reza to SpaceNews, 20 December 2017: **"In 1999, we entered the first cost-sharing program
with the Air Force Research Laboratory."**

Aerojet Rocketdyne release, 6 September 2016, verbatim: the tests "marked the first use of
the **Mondaloy 200 super alloy, which was developed jointly by Aerojet Rocketdyne and the
AFRL Materials Directorate**."

**Three distinct AFRL elements are in play, and keeping them apart is the whole of the next
section:**

| Element | Location | Role in Mondaloy |
|---|---|---|
| AFRL **Materials Directorate** | Wright-Patterson AFB, Ohio | Named in the 2016 release as the government co-developer |
| AFRL **Rocket Propulsion Division** | Edwards AFB, California | Ran Hydrocarbon Boost testing |
| AFRL **Space Vehicles Directorate** | Kirtland AFB, New Mexico | **No documented role.** This is McCasland's early-2000s posting |

**Contract vehicle not established.** No contract number, task order or dollar value found
for either the 1999 cost-share or Hydrocarbon Boost. DTIC returned 403. NASA NTRS returned
**zero** results for "mondaloy" against an API sanity-checked as working ("inconel" returns
605).

## The McCasland question

What is actually alleged, from the Oversight letter to FBI Director Patel, 20 April 2026,
read in full:

> "Reports have even alleged a direct link between Ms. Reza and General McCasland
> characterizing them as having a 'close professional connection' through an Air
> Force-funded research program in the early 2000s pertaining to 'advanced materials needed
> for reusable space vehicles and weapons.'"

Footnote 5 attributes this to Shane Galvin, N.Y. Post, 21 March 2026. **The letter makes no
congressional finding** and describes its whole basis as "unconfirmed public reporting."

The Post's own basis, read in full, is one sentence of the reporter's own inference from a
public service biography: that McCasland's Kirtland posting "would directly pertain to
Reza's research." **That is an assertion, not a document.**

### Finding

**The timelines do not overlap in the way the allegation requires.**

1. The "early 2000s Air Force-funded research program" invoked is the cost-share that began
   in **1999**. McCasland was Chief Engineer of the GPS Joint Program Office then, and held
   **no AFRL post at all** until October 2001.
2. When he did hold one, it was **Space Vehicles at Kirtland, New Mexico**. Mondaloy's
   documented government partner is the **Materials Directorate at Wright-Patterson, Ohio**.
   The Post names the wrong directorate.
3. The only genuine overlap is **May 2011 to July 2013**, commanding the whole AFRL
   enterprise. **Commanding a laboratory a decade after a programme began is not overseeing
   that programme**, and it is a materially different claim from the one being made.
4. **No document was found placing them on the same programme, contract, review board,
   panel, publication, award or facility at any time.** Searched for specifically.

**The honest characterisation is a shared agency at different times and different levels,
and no documented programmatic link. If the pattern this project tracks is real, it will
need to rest on something other than this connection.**

## A circulating claim that is false on the face of the document

An X post asserts the Mondaloy patent is `US20170082070A1`, co-invented with Hardwick and
qualified by AFRL "under McCasland 2011-2013". That patent was checked directly. It is
**"Turbopump with a single piece housing and a smooth enamel glass surface", Florida Turbine
Technologies, inventors Miller, Pinera, Brooks, Appleby and Leonard.** None of them is
Hardwick. It merely mentions Mondaloy as a candidate coating. **The claim is false.**

## A note on proportion

Two of the three people in this record are gone. Dallis Hardwick died in 2015. Monica
Jacinto Reza has been missing since 22 June 2025. The strongest thing the documentary record
supports about her is the least sensational: she spent nearly thirty years on one hard
materials problem, solved it, and spent two decades getting the solution into hardware.
**"Keeping it funded continuously since the mid-90s, I'd consider one of our team's biggest
feats,"** she told SpaceNews. That is what the record shows.
