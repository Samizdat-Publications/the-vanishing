## Why it fails

There *was* a real, sustained, multi-year campaign to destroy his professional standing, and
it is documented in detail. **But its author was Philip J. Klass, a private Aviation Week
editor acting on his own initiative.** Every state actor in the sequence fails to be one:

| Actor | What the record actually shows |
|---|---|
| **Klass** | Private journalist, personal vendetta, no agency direction documented. An actor, not a programme |
| **ONR / Navy** | Audited him at Klass's instigation and **exonerated him** (9 Jan 1969: "acted within the terms and conditions of his contract"). Then let the contract lapse. A funder declining to renew is the most ordinary event in academic research |
| **FBI** | Opened file 105-191677 under an "INTERNAL SECURITY - RUSSIA" caption, then **declined to interview him** because it "could possibly result in embarrassment to the Bureau." Its own sources vouched his loyalty and confirmed **no classified access**. The file monitors. Nothing in it acts |
| **Nixon White House** | Requested an FBI name check 18 to 19 Mar 1971, the day after the SST vote. **But the transmittal says the request covered "and some other individuals" with seven enclosures.** He was one name in a batch. No document shows the product being used |
| **Condon** | Demanded a document back and phoned a university president. The "tried to get him fired" claim is an **unverified allegation** from a partisan biography, absent from the scholarly account |

**The frame's central move does not survive contact.** Bennewitz works as precedent because
William Moore **publicly admitted the operation in July 1989**. Here there is no admission,
no memo, no directive, no defector. The gap between "a man was ruined" and "a man was ruined
by design" is the whole distance, and nothing crosses it.

**The collapse was also only partial.** He held his tenured chair to his death. UA defended
him. The Navy cleared him. Rep. Yates defended him by name on the House floor. The AMS
obituary is warm.

## The correction that matters most

**The tracker's anchor story for this lead is wrong.** The circulating version is that
**Rep. Silvio Conte**, protecting SST jobs, destroyed McDonald at the hearing by mocking
"little green men." Three problems:

1. **The phrase appears nowhere in the 17 March 1971 Congressional Record.** The full day's
   House proceedings were searched for "green men", "flying object", "Mars" and "UFO".
2. The documented ridicule was in **floor debate, not the hearing**, and by **Rep. Frank T.
   Bow (R-OH)** and **Rep. Donald D. Clancy (R-OH)**, not Conte.
3. **Conte voted against the SST.** The finding aid to his papers at UMass Amherst (mums371)
   says he "voted against supersonic transport (SST) despite widespread support in First
   District." The stated motive is contradicted by his own archive.

A hostile reader who checks the Record before the tracker does would use this to discredit
everything around it.

## What to salvage: a mechanism citation, not a case

The 17 March 1971 exchange is the cleanest primary-source instance of **credibility
destruction as a working political method**. Congressional Record, House, 92nd Cong. 1st
Sess., pp. 6829-6830, retrieved and read directly.

Bow, discounting McDonald's SST ozone testimony by reciting his UFO views: *"Now I cannot
give much credence to the testimony of that expert on skin cancer and ozone."* Rep. Sidney
Yates objected that administration witnesses said McDonald "has the very highest
qualifications as an atmospheric scientist." **Yates was ignored.** The House voted 215-204
the next day.

That belongs in the same slot the Robertson Panel's 1953 debunking recommendation occupies.
**It has no value as a case.**

## Death, stated plainly

Shot himself in the head **circa 9 April 1971** and survived, blinded by optic nerve damage.
Committed to a Tucson psychiatric ward, recovered some peripheral vision, planned to return
to teaching. Body found **13 June 1971** near the Cañada del Oro Wash north of Tucson, with a
.38 revolver and a note. Ruled suicide. He was 51.

Date conflict on the first attempt: 9 April, "April", and "May" all appear. Unresolved.
**No medical examiner report, death certificate or case number was obtained, and none is
cited. No source disputes the ruling on forensic grounds.**
