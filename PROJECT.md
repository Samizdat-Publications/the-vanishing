## Editorial Stance

This tracker is built on the working hypothesis that a pattern exists. Its purpose is to document that pattern and surface connections not visible at the individual case level.

The reasoning behind that stance: individual cases are, by design, unremarkable. A man being shot is not a pattern. A nuclear-adjacent researcher whose stalker was arrested with a loaded unregistered rifle, released after three days, had his charges dropped, and returned eleven days later to complete the killing is a different kind of fact. The claim is about aggregate structure and about circumstances that individually read as prosaic.

That frame has documented historical grounding (see **Historical Precedent** below). It is not the same as asserting that any specific case here was a covert operation, and this tracker does not make that assertion about any individual.

**The skeptical literature is used as a grading instrument, not a rebuttal section.** Every case carries an evidentiary tier. The statistical arguments are what separate the cases that survive hostile scrutiny from the ones that do not. This serves the investigation: it tells a reader where to spend attention, and it denies a hostile critic the move of picking off the weakest entry to discredit the whole set.

### The Scoring Model

The central analytical problem this tracker exists to solve: **an event that reads as ordinary in isolation may not be ordinary in sequence.** A scientist shot by a disturbed neighbor is a crime story. A scientist shot by a disturbed neighbor whom he had reported, who was arrested with a loaded unregistered rifle, released after three days, and had his charges dropped eleven days before the killing is a different object entirely. Nothing in that chain is invented. It only becomes visible when the chain is reconstructed.

A single grade cannot express this, because two different things can make a case interesting and they are independent. So every case carries two scores plus a composite tier.

**Axis 1 -- Sequence Anomaly (S).** How many independently documented, individually unexplained links exist in the chain around the event?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **S3** | Three or more independently documented odd links forming a chain. The sequence is the finding. |
| **S2** | Two documented unexplained elements. |
| **S1** | One unexplained element. |
| **S0** | No anomalous sequence. A conventional account covers the facts. |

**Axis 2 -- Personal Program Proximity (P).** What access did this individual personally hold?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **P3** | Active access to classified, compartmented, nuclear, propulsion, or UAP-adjacent programs at the time of the event. |
| **P2** | Former or adjacent access, or work in a sensitive domain without documented program access. |
| **P1** | Scientific or technical role with no sensitive access documented. |
| **P0** | No defense, aerospace, nuclear, or intelligence nexus of any kind. |

**Axis 3 -- Institutional Channel (I).** Does the person's institution have a documented history of serving as a conduit for classified government work?

This axis exists because personal clearance is the wrong test for civilian academics. The US government has recruited university scientists into classified advisory and consulting work for over sixty years, frequently without the arrangement being publicly attached to the individual. **JASON** -- formed in 1960, administered successively by the Institute for Defense Analyses, MITRE, and General Atomics -- staffs cleared civilian academic physicists and astronomers, including multiple Nobel laureates, to advise DoD on classified matters. MKULTRA ran subprojects through 80+ institutions, many of them universities, often without the participants' knowledge. FFRDCs blur the line by design.

An astronomer's core competencies -- faint-signal detection, adaptive optics, image processing, sensor characterization -- transfer directly to overhead reconnaissance. **Absence of a publicly documented clearance is therefore weak evidence of absence of sensitive work**, and the tracker scores institutional channel separately rather than treating a university appointment as disqualifying.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **I3** | FFRDC, national laboratory, documented JASON / IDA / MITRE / classified-advisory lineage, or direct participation in a DARPA or DoD joint program. |
| **I2** | Major research university or NASA center with substantial federal defense or energy funding. |
| **I1** | Research institution with no documented classified channel. |
| **I0** | Private-sector employer with no government research nexus. |

**Reading the axes together.** P asks what the person is known to have held. I asks what the institution could have routed to them without a public record. A P1/I3 case is not a weak case -- it is a case where the personal record is the thing that is missing, which is precisely the condition the historical precedent predicts. It is, however, a *different* claim from a P3 case, and the tracker keeps them distinguishable rather than merging them into a single number that could be accused of being padded.

**Composite Tier.**

| Tier | Rule |
|---|---|
| **A** | At least one specific unexplained fact that no source has accounted for, AND (S2+ or P3 or I3). These are where the case rests. |
| **B** | Genuinely unresolved, but a prosaic explanation is available and has not been ruled out. Worth tracking; not load-bearing. |
| **C** | A conventional explanation is documented and holds. Retained for completeness and to demonstrate the list is not curated. Removing these would weaken the tracker, not strengthen it. |

**The regulatory basis for I3, added 25 August 2026.** The I axis previously rested on the
assertion that FFRDCs and national laboratories are conduits for classified work. It now has a
primary citation. **FAR 35.017**, read directly at `acquisition.gov/far/35.017`, states that an
FFRDC "has access, **beyond that which is common to the normal contractual relationship**, to
Government and supplier data, including sensitive and proprietary data, and to employees and
installations equipment and real property." That is the government's own acquisition regulation
establishing that an FFRDC sits inside a data relationship no ordinary contractor has.

**This matters because it requires no premise.** A reader who rejects everything else about this
tracker still has to accept FAR 35.017. It applies directly to JPL, Caltech IPAC and MIT Lincoln
Laboratory among the cases scored I3.

**Ten of fifteen cases sit at I3.** That figure should be read carefully in both directions. It supports the argument that these individuals worked inside institutions with real classified channels. It also reflects that FFRDCs and national labs employ tens of thousands of people, which is exactly the denominator problem Novella's base-rate analysis raises. **The I axis establishes plausibility of channel, not evidence of use.**

### Where the skeptics are weak (documented for balance)

- **The tracker now supplies the denominator its critics said was missing.** Novella and Hand both argue from expected counts without a comparison group of comparably exposed scientists. The AAWSAP DIRD author cohort is one, it is named and public, and **it does not support the tracker on the count question.** Carrying it is the point: an aggregate claim must be reproducible by someone who does not share the premise.
- **Scientific American concedes its own gap**: it states outright that it is "impossible to find statistics on the expected number of scientists and science-related people who were shot or disappeared within a given time period in the U.S." Its argument is heuristic, not a calculation.
- **Shermer compresses Ning Li**: presents the 2014 collision and the 2021 Alzheimer's death as unrelated. The reporting establishes the collision caused the brain damage that led to the diagnosis.
- **Shermer's ">500,000 missing yearly"** is the all-ages NCIC figure, heavily juvenile and mostly resolved within days, deployed in an argument about adult professionals. Hand's 200,000-adults figure is correct and the two skeptics appear to contradict each other.
- **The Pentagon's early response was thin**: "no active national security investigations" was weak enough that Comer and Burlison said it left "many unanswered questions." Reflexive agency dismissal is not a finding.

## Technical Details of Current Tracker (v1)

### Stack
- Single-file HTML (1233 lines)
- TypeUI "Levels" design system (CSS custom properties)
- Fonts: Inter + JetBrains Mono (Google Fonts)
- SVG network diagram (viewBox 0 0 1400 900)
- Vanilla JavaScript for filtering and navigation
- No build tools, no dependencies beyond CDN fonts

### Key Features
Stats bar; filter buttons (All/Dead/Missing) via data-status; clickable chronological timeline strip; SVG network crossover diagram; individual case cards with status badges; analysis threads; geographic cluster sections; institution cards grid; counterarguments section; open questions; source URLs in footer.

### CSS Architecture
Custom properties for all colors, spacing, shadows, radii. Card-based layout with hover states. Responsive grid (3-col to 2-col to 1-col). Status-based coloring (red=dead, amber=missing, violet=institution). Monospace date columns.

### JavaScript
- `filterCards(status)`: shows/hides case cards by data-status
- `scrollToCard(id)`: smooth scrolls to a case card by ID

### v2 requirements (see CLAUDE-DESIGN-PROMPT.md)

**Schema change required.** Every case now carries:

| Field | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `tier` | `"A" \| "B" \| "C"` | Composite evidentiary grade |
| `sequenceScore` | `0-3` | Axis 1, anomalous sequence links |
| `significanceScore` | `0-3` | Axis 2, personal program proximity |
| `channelScore` | `0-3` | Axis 3, institutional channel |
| `sequenceChain` | `ChainLink[]` | Ordered, dated, individually sourced steps. **Required for any case scoring S2 or higher.** |
| `tierRationale` | `string` | Why this tier, in one sentence |
| `unexplainedResidual` | `string \| null` | The specific fact no source accounts for |

`ChainLink` is `{ date, event, sourceUrl, isAnomalous }`.

**The UI must support:**
1. Filtering and sorting by each axis independently, not just by tier
2. A **sequence chain timeline** on any S2+ case card -- this is the Grillmair view and it is the single most important new component
3. A scatter or matrix view plotting S against P, with I encoded as size or color, so the reader sees at a glance which cases occupy which quadrant
4. Tier and all three scores surfaced on the card face, not buried in a detail panel

This is not cosmetic. The scoring model is the mechanism that makes the case list defensible, and the sequence chain is the mechanism that makes cases like Grillmair legible at all.
